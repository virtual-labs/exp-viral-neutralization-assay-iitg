### Procedure

#### Materials Required
* Virus stock of known infectivity
* Serum samples (test and control)
* Susceptible cell culture (e.g., Vero cells)
* Culture medium
* Sterile microtiter plates
* \(\text{CO}_2\) incubator
* Micropipettes and sterile tips

#### Step-by-Step Procedure

1. **Serum Preparation**
   The serum samples are first heat-inactivated at 56°C for about 30 minutes. This step is necessary to inactivate complement proteins that could otherwise influence the outcome of the assay.

2. **Serial Dilution of Serum**
   The serum is diluted in a series (e.g., two-fold dilutions) using sterile medium in a microtiter plate. This helps in determining the antibody concentration required for neutralization.

3. **Addition of Virus**
   A constant amount of virus is added to each dilution of serum. Controls must be included:
   * **Virus control** (virus without serum)
   * **Cell control** (cells without virus)

4. **Incubation of Virus–Serum Mixture**
   The mixture is incubated, typically at 37°C for about 1 hour, to allow sufficient interaction between the virus and antibodies.

5. **Inoculation of Cell Culture**
   The incubated mixtures are then transferred onto a monolayer of susceptible cells grown in microtiter plates.

6. **Post-infection Incubation**
   The plates are incubated for several days (usually 2–5 days), depending on the virus used.

7. **Observation of Cytopathic Effect (CPE)**
   The cells are examined under a microscope:
   * Healthy, intact cells indicate successful neutralization.
   * Damaged or lysed cells indicate viral infection.

8. **Determination of Neutralization Titer**
   The highest dilution of serum that completely prevents cytopathic effect is recorded as the neutralization titer.
